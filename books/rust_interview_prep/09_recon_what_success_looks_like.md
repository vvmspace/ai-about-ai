# What Success Looks Like

Early in my career, I entered interviews with one objective: be flawless.
That objective had looked ambitious.
In practice, it was sabotage wearing a good suit.

Flawlessness is not measurable in a live conversation.
Performance is.
So I define success by round, using explicit pass criteria.

For recruiter round, success means:
- I can explain why this role fits my trajectory in under 90 seconds;
- I can discuss compensation and logistics without awkward fog;
- I can ask two precise questions that prove I read the room.

For hiring-manager round, success means:
- I reason about ownership, reliability, and delivery trade-offs;
- I narrate one incident with clear structure and measurable outcome;
- I demonstrate cross-stack judgment, not role silo preference.

For Rust technical round, success means:
- correctness-first thinking before micro-optimisation;
- explicit handling of errors and backpressure;
- clear explanation when I choose channels, locks, or bounded queues.

For system design round, success means:
- requirements clarified before architecture theatre;
- critical path and bottlenecks identified early;
- latency variance and failure modes treated as first-class concerns.

For behavioural round, success means:
- concise stories with ownership and consequences;
- no hero mythology, no blame transfer;
- reflective learning that sounds practical, not ceremonial.

I also define two universal standards for every round.

Standard one: language hygiene.
I avoid filler and vague abstractions.
If I say “fast,” I attach a metric.
If I say “reliable,” I attach a failure policy.

Standard two: recovery quality.
Mistakes are allowed.
Unstructured recovery is not.
If I stumble, I restate assumptions, narrow scope, and continue.

The beautiful side effect is emotional.
When success is specific, anxiety loses vocabulary.
I know exactly what I am trying to do.

Before interview day, I print the criteria on one sheet and score myself after mocks from 1 to 5.
Anything below 4 gets targeted rehearsal.
No drama, just iteration.

Interviews reward clear standards the way production systems reward clear SLOs.
Define what good looks like, and your behaviour will follow.
The room stops feeling random once your scoreboard is honest.
