# After the Handshake

Most candidates treat the offer as the finish line.
I treat it as a transfer of responsibility.
The interview measured potential.
The next ninety days measure execution.

I keep two plans ready before the final call.
Plan A if hired.
Plan B if rejected.
Agency is easier when prepared in advance.

If hired, I run a 30/60/90 structure.
Not bureaucratic.
Operational.
It keeps momentum visible and reduces onboarding drift.

First 30 days: map the system and trust boundaries.
I read architecture docs and incident reports.
I shadow order-path changes from commit to production.
I identify where latency and correctness trade-offs are decided.
I set up local profiling workflows and dashboards on day one.

I also schedule focused conversations.
Trading lead.
Infra owner.
Frontend counterpart.
Risk stakeholder.
I ask each for one failure they never want repeated.
That answer tells me where political and technical sensitivity overlap.

By day 60, I ship small but meaningful improvements.
A measurable latency variance reduction.
A clearer API contract.
A runbook fix that cuts incident triage time.
Early wins should be boringly useful.
No fireworks.
Just credibility.

I maintain a weekly evidence log.
Changes shipped.
Metrics moved.
Risks discovered.
Decisions deferred.
This log later supports performance reviews and protects memory from narrative drift.

By day 90, I aim for partial ownership of a critical path.
Not hero ownership.
Sustainable ownership.
I can explain failure modes, rollback paths, and capacity limits without guessing.
That is when trust becomes durable.

If rejected, I do not romanticize it.
I run a post-mortem within twenty-four hours.
Which rounds felt weak?
Which questions exposed gaps?
Which stories lacked evidence?
Then I convert pain into backlog.

My rejection backlog has three columns.
Knowledge gap.
Communication gap.
Signal gap.
Knowledge means I did not know.
Communication means I knew but presented poorly.
Signal means I answered without proving relevance.
Each column gets a different training method.

I then update artifacts.
CV bullet precision.
Interview notebook from [Building My Prep Notebook](./08_recon_building_my_prep_notebook.md).
Portfolio examples.
Mock answers.
The next process should inherit improvements, not repeat turbulence.

There is a psychological advantage to this approach.
Outcomes stop feeling like verdicts on identity.
They become feedback on strategy and execution.
That mindset protects energy over long careers.

I close every cycle with one sentence in my notes.
What did this process teach me about my craft?
If the sentence is honest, I am already stronger than when I started.

An offer is useful.
A refined operating method is better.
One pays this quarter.
The other compounds.
